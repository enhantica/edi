# Co₂SiO₄ on D20

The same staged fit as the Python and Command line tutorials, in the app. Its structure and experiment come from EasyDiffraction's collection with **Load ▾ From web…**, and each stage frees its parameters in the **Parameters** table on **Analysis** before the fit.

This tutorial fits the olivine Co₂SiO₄ to constant-wavelength neutron powder data measured on
D20 at the ILL. Its structure and its measured pattern come from EasyDiffraction's collection;
the tutorial describes the experiment, links the two and fits the coordinates, the
displacements and the oxygen occupancies.

## Scale, 2θ zero and cell

The scale sets the overall height of the pattern, and the zero shift and the three cell lengths put the peaks where they are measured.

Filter the **Cell** parameters using the category filter, click the **Free** column header and choose **Free · all**; then tick **Free** for the **Linked structures** rows the stage names; then tick **Free** for the **Instrument** rows the stage names, then press **Fit** in the status bar.

## Atom positions

The peak intensities now depend mostly on where the atoms are. Co1 sits on a centre of symmetry and the other sites on the mirror plane, so only their free coordinates are fitted.

## Constrain the two Co sites

The correlations show no strong pair for the two Co sites, yet the fit gives them very different displacements, Co1's even negative, which no atom can have: both are Co and scatter alike, so the pattern cannot tell their thermal motions apart. Give each an alias and constrain Co2's to follow Co1's, starting again from 0.5 Å², then fit again; the shared displacement is positive and its s.u. smaller than either had alone.

Tick **Free** for the **Atomic displacement** rows the stage names, then press **Fit** in the status bar.
